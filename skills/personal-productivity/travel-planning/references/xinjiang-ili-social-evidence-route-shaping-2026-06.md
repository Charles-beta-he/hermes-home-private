# Xinjiang Ili social-evidence route shaping (2026-06)

## Applicability
Use for mainland China self-drive travel planning when the user wants a route shaped by social/UGC evidence (Xiaohongshu, Dianping/Meituan, Gaode/Baidu, OTA notes) rather than a generic itinerary, especially for Xinjiang/Ili-style long-distance scenic loops.

Anti-conditions:
- Do not reuse the named ranking as live truth without rechecking current posts, weather, tickets, road controls, and lodging.
- Do not treat a user-specified “must visit” as “allocate a full day.” Must-visit means “route must include it”; duration still depends on value density, ticket/traffic friction, and opportunity cost.
- Do not claim Xiaohongshu evidence was read if web login/search was blocked; label the boundary and request screenshots or use alternative evidence.

## Session-derived pattern
A user planning Hangzhou → Xinjiang, 4 people / two couples, rental SUV, 2 drivers, 5–6h driving tolerance, hotel 400–700 CNY/room/night, required Sayram Lake and Nalati, interested in Duku Highway, corrected the agent: “do not give a full day just because I specified must-go; first confirm treasure spots and rank playable points.”

Reusable steps:
1. Convert “must-go” into a route inclusion constraint, not a time allocation rule.
2. Build a candidate pool from:
   - required anchors;
   - likely high-value scenic points;
   - social/UGC “treasure spot” candidates;
   - food/city windows;
   - road-experience segments such as Duku.
3. Rank candidates by:
   - scenery intensity / “not wasted trip” value;
   - route fit and backtracking cost;
   - couple/friend experience quality;
   - peak-season crowd/ticket/traffic risk;
   - time value: 2h / half-day / big half-day / full-day.
4. Allocate time from the ranking, not from name recognition. Example: Sayram Lake can be a big half-day; Nalati can be half-day to big half-day depending on self-drive ticket; XiaTa or Qiongkushitai may outrank them in experience value.
5. Preserve city food windows when the trip objective includes food, not just scenery. For Ili routes, Yining can be a required food/city-experience anchor even if it is not a “scenic spot.”
6. Treat road-experience items (Duku Highway) as risk-managed travel days, not ordinary POIs. Keep them off the final flight day and define weather/closure/traffic gates.

## Web/social evidence fallback
If Xiaohongshu web search is blocked or returns empty after login:
- Do not keep fighting the same UI indefinitely.
- State the evidence boundary explicitly.
- Ask the user for mobile-app screenshots of result pages or notes for OCR.
- Meanwhile use accessible sources (official/scenic-area pages, OTA guide pages, maps, Wikipedia/TravelChinaGuide for geography/seasonality) to produce a provisional ranking.
- Mark provisional vs confirmed UGC evidence separately.

## Ili/Xinjiang provisional ranking example
For a 9-day Hangzhou→Urumqi loop with Sayram Lake, Nalati, and Duku interest:
1. XiaTa / Zhaosu — high scenic density if weather cooperates.
2. Sayram Lake — include as a big half-day, not necessarily a full day.
3. Qiongkushitai / Kalajun — choose by lodging/road reliability; Qiongkushitai for atmosphere, Kalajun for stability.
4. Duku Highway + Bayinbuluk — high-value road day with hard safety gates.
5. Nalati — half-day to big half-day; self-drive ticket increases value.
6. Yining food/city night — required if “food + scenery” is the objective.
7. Chaxi / Kuerdening / Tangbula — alternate treasure pool; use if current UGC/map evidence beats the mainline or weather disrupts XiaTa/Nalati.

## Verification checklist before booking
- Flights avoid final-day early departure after a mountain-road segment.
- Rental SUV capacity for 4 people + luggage, insurance, rescue, route restrictions.
- Nalati self-drive ticket / shuttle policy.
- XiaTa opening, shuttle, latest entry, hiking/weather conditions.
- Qiongkushitai lodging, road condition, parking, and whether a same-day transfer to Nalati is realistic.
- Duku opening, weather, rockfall/traffic controls, and a fallback route to Urumqi.
