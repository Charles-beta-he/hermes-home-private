# Medical misconduct / sexual-assault public-evidence research

Use when a user asks whether a named hospital, doctor, procedure, or medical setting has public sexual assault / molestation / privacy-violation allegations or confirmed cases.

Applicability: public-source research only. This is not legal or medical advice, and it does not establish whether an unindexed event did or did not happen.

Anti-conditions: do not use this file to investigate private individuals beyond public-interest medical-professional allegations; do not infer guilt from allegations; do not infer safety from search-result absence.

## Goal

Separate: (1) confirmed cases, (2) allegations with official/police/court follow-up, (3) media/social reposts without closure, and (4) unrelated similarly-worded regional cases.

## Search procedure

1. Query exact entity + misconduct terms:
   - `"<hospital>" "猥亵"`
   - `"<hospital>" "性侵"`
   - `"<hospital>" "侵犯" "医生"`
   - `"<hospital>" "女患者" "医生" "猥亵"`
   - For procedure-specific concerns: `"<hospital>" "胃肠镜" "麻醉" "猥亵"`, `"<hospital>" "无痛胃肠镜" "性侵"`.
2. Search the institution site separately for both procedure and misconduct keywords. Institution-site hits are useful for services/protocols and for ruling out obvious official notices, but absence is not proof.
3. Search broad web/news/social-indexed sources. If one search engine degrades or CAPTCHA appears, switch engine; do not over-query one engine.
4. Classify every hit by entity match:
   - exact hospital / exact doctor / exact procedure;
   - same province or city but different institution;
   - generic anesthesia case used as analogy;
   - medical-advice page or SEO filler.
5. Prefer official/police/court/major-media sources for claims. Treat self-media reposts, short-video pages, and SEO snippets as leads only.

## Reporting format

- Start with the narrow answer: `I did/did not find a public credible case matching <entity + scenario>.`
- Then list evidence buckets:
  - exact-match findings;
  - related-but-not-same findings;
  - procedure/service facts from official site;
  - practical safety questions to ask.
- Avoid implying exoneration from absence of indexed results. Say `I did not find public evidence in the sources checked`, not `nothing happened`.
- For hospitals/procedures, include operational questions the user can ask: who is present, whether a female nurse can be present, anesthesia monitoring records, recovery-room handoff, family handoff, and consent for add-on procedures.

## Pitfalls

- Search snippets often mix same-region cases with the named hospital. Do not transfer allegations to the named institution unless the institution is explicitly named.
- Medical anesthesia cases can be real crimes, unresolved allegations, or anesthesia/sedation perception disputes. Do not collapse them into one narrative.
- Explicit sexual-misconduct queries are sensitive: keep wording factual, avoid sensational detail, and distinguish legal status (`alleged`, `reported`, `police said`, `court found`, `convicted`, `acquitted`).
- If the user is deciding whether to undergo a procedure, convert findings into concrete process checks rather than fear amplification.
